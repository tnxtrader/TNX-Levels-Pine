T3 ENTRY LAB — BUILD FRAMEWORK

Every candidate in the 5000-row registry, on every instrument, through one fixed sequence of phases. A candidate must first show a real timing edge before costs, then prove it is not luck and not time in the market, then survive costs, stress and time, and only then face the Validation and the sealed Test windows, each opened once. A phase starts only when the previous phase closed with PASS; a phase that closes with STOP ends the program, and that is a valid result.

THE LOCKED PILLARS

1EDGE FIRST, THEN COSTS

A unit is first judged before costs: its gross result must beat the drift of simply being in the market, significantly, after the multiple-testing correction, with favourable excursions larger than adverse ones and persistence across time; its timing must beat the same signals placed at random times. Only an edge that exists before costs is then required to survive costs, cost stress and slippage stress. Every acceptance decision after P2 is made on net results.

2NEXT-OPEN EXECUTION, CONSERVATIVE FILLS, FLAT BY SESSION END

A signal is known at the close of bar t and filled at the open of bar t+1. When stop and target are both inside one bar, the stop wins. A gap through a level fills at the open. No position is held across a session end or a contract roll.

3INSTRUMENTS ARE INDEPENDENT

The unit of research is candidate × instrument: 5000 × 5 = 25,000 units. Evidence is never pooled across instruments.

4EVERY TRIAL COUNTED, EVERY WINDOW OPENED ONCE, POWER CHECKED FIRST

All 25,000 units and every parameter set tried enter the effective-trial count behind the deflation statistics. Validation is opened once (P5) and Test once (P6), each only after the minimum track record length shows the window is long enough to confirm the edge. Nothing seen in them changes any choice.

5ATR AND TICKS, NEVER PERCENT

Results are in ticks and in RATR = result ÷ ATR(14) of the signal bar. The data is back-adjusted additively and can be negative, so percentage returns are never used.

PART A — THE ARCHITECTURE

A.1 — The governing principle

A unit earns trust only by surviving every gate in order. One failed hard gate removes it from every later phase. The program narrows: 25,000 units → shortlist (≤ 50) → refined → evidence survivors → validation survivors → test survivors → live. Parameters are chosen only in P3, on Dev training blocks; everywhere else they are frozen.

A.2 — The gate law

  1. Every gate is a fixed rule with a fixed threshold written in config.yaml before P2 runs.
  2. Unit gates are columns g_* in the phase table; a unit continues only if all of them are true.
  3. Each phase writes gates.json and closes PASS (its phase gate holds) or STOP. The next phase refuses to start on STOP.

PART B — STANDING LAWS

B.1 — Never changes

  1. Raw files are never modified; repairs are made on the loaded copy and counted in P0.
  2. config.yaml and the registry are frozen once P2 has run (B.20).
  3. Validation and Test results never change a parameter, a gate or a candidate list.
  4. Ranking is never on win rate or profit factor; it is on t-statistics of the per-session series.
  5. No filter, session or date restriction is added after seeing a losing period; a different rule is a different candidate and goes through P2.
  6. The in-sample equity of the final parameters on Dev is never evidence; evidence is walk-forward out-of-sample (P4), Validation (P5) and Test (P6).
  7. A single best grid cell is never chosen: choices are plateau-smoothed (B.12) and neighbours must work (B.16).

B.2 — The execution law

  1. Signal: computed from bars ≤ t, evaluated at the close of bar t.
  2. Entry: market order at the open of bar e = t + 1 (t + 2 in the delay stress).
  3. No entry when bar e is in another session or contract than bar t, beyond the window, or when ATR[t] is not positive.
  4. One position at a time: signals while a trade is open are ignored; a signal on the exit bar is allowed.
  5. Shorts run on mirrored prices (o, h, l, c) → (−o, −l, −h, −c) and −T3, so every long rule has an exact short twin.
  6. Sessions: a CME session starts 17:00 CT (session date = timestamp + 7 h); a gap ≥ 120 minutes also starts a session. A roll is a change of the contract column.
Basis: next-bar execution removes look-ahead [R10]; CME Globex trading day [R22].

B.3 — The barrier law (stop, target, time, session end)

ATR      = Wilder ATR(14) of the signal bar t
SL ticks = max(1, round(SL_ATR × ATR / tick))        stop   = entry − SL ticks × tick
TP ticks = max(1, round(TP_R × SL ticks))            target = entry + TP ticks × tick
last bar = min(e + max_bars − 1, last bar of the session, last bar before a roll)
  1. Stop and target are fixed at the fill.
  2. For each bar j from e to the last bar:
    • j > e and open ≤ stop → SL at the open; j > e and open ≥ target → TP at the open.
    • low ≤ stop and high ≥ target in one bar → SL at the stop (intrabar: pessimistic); with intrabar: tv the TradingView path decides (open nearer the high → high first → TP).
    • only low ≤ stop → SL at the stop; only high ≥ target → TP at the target.
  3. No barrier by bar e + max_bars − 1 → TIME at the open of bar e + max_bars, if it is in the same session and contract.
  4. Session or contract ends first → EOS at the close of its last bar.
  5. SL_ATR = 0: no stop, no target. With max_bars = 8 this is the screen exit of P2.
gross R_ATR = (exit − entry) / ATR
net ticks   = (exit − entry) / tick − cost_ticks_rt − (stop_slip_ticks if SL)
net R_ATR   = net ticks × tick / ATR
MFE_ATR     = max over held bars of (min(high, target) − entry) / ATR
MAE_ATR     = max over held bars of (entry − max(low, stop)) / ATR
Basis: ATR [R18]; ATR-scaled stops and R-multiples, MFE / MAE [R13, R14]; stop-first tie rule and gap fills are the conservative bar-data convention; TradingView path [R21].

B.9 — The registry law

  1. registry/candidates.csv: candidate_id, family, trigger, filter, side, t3_length, t3_vf; T3 settings are present exactly when the trigger or the filter uses T3.
  2. P1 writes the executable registry: uses_t3, cell_id = trigger | filter | side, trigger_def, filter_def (B.7, B.8) and spec_hash = sha1 of (trigger, filter, side, T3 length, T3 vf, rules version).
  3. The registry is frozen in P1. A new idea is a new row and a new program from P2.

B.11 — The ledger and the effective-trial law

  1. P2 records all 25,000 units in P2/units.csv, passed or not.
  2. P3 records per unit the number of configs evaluated, their effective number Keff(configs), and their Dev series for the PBO.
  3. The DSR of a unit uses N = Keff(instrument universe) + Keff(the unit's configs): the effective number of independent trials behind its figure.
Basis: effective number of trials by clustering [R4]; DSR [R2].

B.12 — The search law (T3, stop, target, hold)

  1. Objective: 25th percentile, over the training blocks, of the block's mean net RATR per session; one training block → its mean; fewer than 30 training trades → −∞.
  2. Stage A — T3 (T3 units only): L × v = 12 × 6 = 72, each with the screen exit.
  3. Stage B — exits on the Stage-A setting: SL_ATR {0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0} × TP_R {0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0} × max bars {4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32} = 252.
  4. Plateau rule: each grid score is replaced by the mean of the finite scores in its 3 × 3 (A) or 3 × 3 × 3 (B) neighbourhood; the highest smoothed score wins.
  5. Optuna (search: optuna): TPE sampler, 200 trials on the joint space, same objective; all trials enter the PBO and Keff(configs).
  6. No machine-learning model is fitted in this program.
Basis: walk-forward selection [R10, R20]; robust (lower-quantile) objective and plateau selection against overfitting [R3, R23]; Optuna TPE [R25].

B.13 — The edge-before-costs law

  1. Drift: the gross result of a unit is compared with the gross result of the same exit entered after every eligible bar of the same window and side. Only the excess is edge; the rest is time in the market.
  2. Timing: the unit's gross per-session mean must beat its random-timing null (same signals rolled by whole sessions, same parameters, same prices; keeps signal count, clustering and time of day).
  3. Entry quality: edge ratio > 1.
  4. Persistence: positive excess in ≥ 4 of 6 Dev blocks.
  5. Costs are applied after these questions are answered; a unit needs both.
Basis: detrending and randomization testing of rules [R11, R12]; edge ratio [R13, R14].

B.14 — The Sharpe convention

SR = mean ÷ standard deviation of the per-session series (zeros on no-trade sessions), per session, not annualised. The same definition is used in P2, P4, P5 and P6.

Basis: [R15, R1].

B.17 — The power law (no window is opened that cannot confirm)

  1. Before Validation: the MinTRL of the unit's walk-forward out-of-sample series must not exceed the number of Validation sessions (P4).
  2. Before Test: the MinTRL of the unit's Validation series must not exceed the number of Test sessions (P5).
Basis: minimum track record length [R1].

B.18 — The closeout and visual evidence law

  1. Each phase writes out/<phase>/report.md with five sections: What this phase did · What was tried · What was changed · What was found · Gates, followed by its plots.
  2. It writes gates.json, its tables as CSV and the plots listed in its Outputs box.
  3. The report is read before the next phase starts.

B.19 — The verification law

  1. Acceptance tests (run in P1 and by python -m pytest tests): the engine and the drift baseline equal slow reference implementations written from B.2–B.3; all 60 rules equal a slow reference written from B.7–B.8; shorts mirror longs exactly; no rule reads future bars; T3 and ATR equal slow references; Newey-West t and Benjamini-Hochberg equal statsmodels; the expected-maximum-SR formula, the null SR variance and the DSR false-positive rate match Monte Carlo; MinTRL is exactly where PSR reaches 95%; PBO, the stationary bootstrap, SPA and Keff behave correctly on known inputs.
  2. End-to-end check (python run.py verify): on a pure random walk the program must stop without a survivor; on a random walk with a planted breakout edge, the planted units must pass P6 and nothing else may pass.
  3. Both must pass before P0 is run on the real data.

B.20 — The amendment and seal law

  1. Changing any setting, cost, gate, rule or registry row after P2 has run starts a new program: new out_dir, rerun from P2, the old folder kept.
  2. P6 writes a LOCK holding the hash of the units it opened the Test window for. A later P6 on a different unit set is refused; forcing it fails every Test gate.

P0 — DATA, SESSIONS & WINDOWS

Job

Load and certify the five 30-minute files, build sessions and rolls, cut the windows and seal the Test rows. Nothing is tested.

Inputs

t3lab/data/: one CSV per instrument with timestamp, open, high, low, close, volume, contract (exchange time, CT).

Steps

  1. Load each file and sort by time.
  2. Duplicates: remove repeated timestamps (keep the last) and count them.
  3. OHLC containment: count bars with high < max(open, close) or low > min(open, close); set high = max(o, h, l, c), low = min(o, h, l, c).
  4. Grids: share of timestamps on the 30-minute grid; share of prices on the tick grid.
  5. Sessions (B.2): session date = timestamp + 7 h; a gap ≥ 120 minutes also starts a session; bars per session and short sessions counted.
  6. Rolls: flag contract changes; compare the median |open − previous close| / ATR at roll opens with normal session opens.
  7. Windows (A.3): Dev 60% / 1 embargo session / Validation 20% / 1 embargo session / Test 20%; Dev cut into 6 equal blocks.
  8. Seal: SHA-256 of the Test rows stored in gates.json; Test rows are dropped on every load before P6.
  9. Cache each instrument with session, roll, window, block and session-bucket columns.
Gates (hard)
  1. OHLC repairs ≤ 0.1% of bars.
  2. Timestamps on the 30-minute grid ≥ 99%.
  3. Prices on the tick grid ≥ 99%.
  4. Every window of every instrument has ≥ 250 sessions.
Outputs — data_qa.csv, repairs.csv, splits.csv; split_timeline.png (price with Validation and Test shaded); sessions.png (bars per session; time-of-day coverage).
Closeout — Forwards the cached, split data. python run.py P0

P1 — REGISTRY & ENGINE ACCEPTANCE

Job

Prove the code obeys the laws exactly, and make every candidate executable. No return is computed.

Steps

  1. Acceptance tests (B.19, 32 tests): hand-made engine cases (target fill, stop-first tie, TradingView path, gap through stop, time exit, session-end exit, one position, costs, delay); engine and drift baseline vs slow references in 12 random configurations on both sides; sessions and rolls; T3 and ATR vs slow references; all 60 rules vs a slow reference; mirror; no look-ahead; every rule reachable; statistics vs statsmodels and Monte Carlo.
  2. Registry checks: missing columns, duplicate ids, unknown triggers, unknown filters, invalid sides, T3 rows without T3 settings, T3 settings out of range, duplicate specifications.
  3. Executable registry (B.9): registry.csv with uses_t3, cell_id, trigger_def, filter_def, spec_hash.
  4. Trigger overlap: Jaccard overlap of the 32 triggers' entry sets on Dev, long side, at the registry's median T3 setting; pairs ≥ 0.5 listed.
Gates (hard)
  1. All acceptance tests pass.
  2. Every registry check counts 0.
  3. Unique spec hashes = number of rows.
Outputs — acceptance_tests.csv, registry_checks.csv, registry.csv, trigger_overlap.csv; registry_composition.png, trigger_overlap.png.
Closeout — Forwards the frozen executable registry. python run.py P1

P2 — SCREEN: EDGE BEFORE COSTS, THEN AFTER COSTS

Job

Run every candidate on every instrument on Dev with one identical screen exit, keep only units with a real edge before costs that also stays significant after costs, and measure the size and difficulty of the search.

Steps

  1. Signals: trigger ∧ filter with the row's own T3 setting, on the row's side.
  2. Screen exit: no stop, no target, TIME at the open of bar entry + 8 (EOS earlier at a session end).
  3. Drift baseline per instrument and side: mean gross RATR of the screen exit entered after every eligible Dev bar (B.13).
  4. Layer A — edge before costs, per unit:
    • trades n; gross mean; excess = gross − baseline per trade;
    • per-session excess series → Newey-West t and one-sided p;
    • edge ratio = mean MFEATR ÷ mean MAEATR;
    • number of Dev blocks with positive excess.
  5. Benjamini-Hochberg over all 25,000 gross p-values.
  6. Layer B — after costs, per unit: net mean RATR per trade; per-session net series → Newey-West t; win rate; SR.
  7. Universe per instrument, on the net per-session series of all its units: Keff; spread of SR; Hansen SPA p (500 draws); PBO of picking the Dev winner (16 blocks).
  8. De-duplication within each instrument: sort passing units by net t; keep a unit only if the Jaccard overlap of its entry bars with every kept unit is ≤ 0.30.
  9. Shortlist: the 50 best remaining units by net t.
  10. Horizon profile of the shortlist: gross excess for time exits of 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 bars.
Gates — per unit (hard, all required)
  1. g_trades: n ≥ 100.
  2. g_edge: mean excess per trade > 0.
  3. g_t_gross: Newey-West t of the excess series ≥ 2.0.
  4. g_bh_gross: BH q ≤ 0.10 over all 25,000 units.
  5. g_e_ratio: edge ratio > 1.0.
  6. g_blocks: excess positive in ≥ 4 of 6 Dev blocks.
  7. g_net: net mean per trade > 0.
  8. g_t_net: Newey-West t of the net series ≥ 2.0.
Gate — phase: shortlist ≥ 1 unit.
Outputs — units.csv (all 25,000 with every figure and gate), universe.csv, gate_funnel.csv, shortlist.csv; screen_distribution.png (gross and net t against N(0,1); gross excess vs net), family_heatmap.png (share with an edge before costs by family × instrument), entry_quality.png (edge ratio vs excess; horizon profile).
Closeout — Forwards the shortlist and the universe figures (Keff, SPA p). python run.py P2

P3 — T3 SETTINGS, STOP, TARGET, HOLD (WALK-FORWARD IN DEV)

Job

For each shortlisted unit, choose the T3 setting and the exit — stop in ATR, target as a multiple of the stop, maximum bars — without ever scoring a choice on the data it was chosen on.

Steps

  1. Folds: fold k = 1…5 trains on blocks B0…B(k−1) and is scored on Bk.
  2. Stage A (T3 units): score the 72 T3 settings with the screen exit on the training blocks (B.12); plateau-smooth; take the best.
  3. Stage B: score the 252 exits on that T3 setting on the training blocks; plateau-smooth; take the best.
  4. Score the fold: apply the chosen parameters unchanged to block Bk; keep its per-session net results and trades.
  5. Stitch B1…B5 into the walk-forward out-of-sample (WF-OOS) series.
  6. Final parameters: Stages A and B on all six blocks; frozen for P4–P7.
  7. Search size: configs evaluated, Keff of their Dev series, and PBO (CSCV, 16 blocks) over them.
Gates — per unit (hard)
  1. g_oos_trades: WF-OOS trades ≥ 50.
  2. g_oos_mean: WF-OOS mean per session > 0.
Gate — phase: ≥ 1 unit passes.
Outputs — refined.csv (final T3 length and vf, SL_ATR, TP_R, max bars, configs evaluated, Keff of configs, PBO, WF-OOS mean and trades, positive folds), wf_folds.csv (choice and result of every fold), units/*.npz; exit_surfaces.png (Dev score over SL × TP at the chosen hold), wf_folds.png.
Closeout — Forwards the passing units with their final parameters and WF-OOS series. python run.py P3

P4 — TIMING, SELECTION BIAS, ROBUSTNESS, STABILITY, POWER

Job

Decide whether each refined unit is real: its timing must beat random timing before costs, its walk-forward out-of-sample result must survive the full search, stress and time, and Validation must be long enough to confirm it.

Steps

  1. Random-timing null on gross R (B.13): final parameters on Dev, 1,000 draws.
  2. Bootstrap the WF-OOS series (2,000 draws): 95% CI and one-sided p.
  3. Deflated Sharpe of the WF-OOS series with N = Keff(universe) + Keff(configs) (B.11).
  4. PBO of the P3 search; SPA p of the instrument's universe.
  5. Fold dispersion: share of positive folds; largest fold ÷ sum of positive folds.
  6. Stress on Dev with the final parameters: cost × 1.5; +1 tick per side; one-bar delay.
  7. Neighbours: every grid axis one step down and up; median Dev mean.
  8. Concentration and time: mean without the best 5% of trades; total without the best year; share of positive years.
  9. Stability (B.15) by year, volatility tercile and session bucket.
  10. Power (B.17): MinTRL of the WF-OOS series vs the number of Validation sessions.
Gates — per unit (hard, all required)
  1. g_null_gross: random-timing null p ≤ 0.05.
  2. g_ci_lo: 95% CI lower bound of the WF-OOS mean > 0.
  3. g_dsr: DSR ≥ 0.95.
  4. g_pbo: PBO ≤ 0.40.
  5. g_spa: SPA p of the universe ≤ 0.10.
  6. g_wf_pos: ≥ 70% of folds positive.
  7. g_wf_share: no fold above 50% of the positive-fold total.
  8. g_cost: mean > 0 at cost × 1.5.
  9. g_slip: mean > 0 with +1 tick per side.
  10. g_delay: mean > 0 with a one-bar delay.
  11. g_neighbours: neighbours' median > 0.
  12. g_top_trim: mean > 0 without the best 5% of trades.
  13. g_best_year: total > 0 without the best year.
  14. g_years: ≥ 60% of years positive.
  15. g_stable: no judged bucket significantly negative; ≤ 1 negative regime bucket; ≤ 1 negative session bucket.
  16. g_power: MinTRL ≤ Validation sessions.
Gate — phase: ≥ 1 unit passes all 16.
Outputs — evidence.csv, gate_funnel.csv, survivors.csv; wf_equity_ci.png (WF-OOS equity; CI forest), gate_matrix.png (units × gates), null_tests.png (random timing vs actual).
Closeout — Forwards the units that passed all 16 gates with frozen parameters. python run.py P4

P5 — VALIDATION (ONE LOOK, PARAMETERS FROZEN)

Job

Run every P4 survivor once on the Validation window with frozen parameters, and confirm the Test window can confirm what Validation shows.

Steps

  1. Load Dev + Validation (Test rows still dropped); build each unit's signal with its frozen T3 setting.
  2. Simulate on Validation with the frozen exit; per-session net series.
  3. Stationary bootstrap (10,000 draws): mean, 95% CI, one-sided p; Benjamini-Hochberg across the P5 units at q = 0.05.
  4. Random-timing null on gross R inside Validation (1,000 draws).
  5. Cost × 1.5 on Validation.
  6. Power (B.17): MinTRL of the Validation series vs the number of Test sessions.
Gates — per unit (hard, all required)
  1. g_trades: ≥ 30 Validation trades.
  2. g_mean: Validation mean per session > 0.
  3. g_bh: BH q ≤ 0.05.
  4. g_null_gross: null p ≤ 0.05.
  5. g_cost: mean > 0 at cost × 1.5.
  6. g_power: MinTRL ≤ Test sessions.
Gate — phase: ≥ 1 unit passes.
Outputs — validation.csv, survivors.csv; validation.png (equity through Dev and Validation; Validation CI forest).
Closeout — Forwards the validation survivors unchanged. python run.py P5

P6 — SEALED TEST (ONE LOOK) & DEPLOYMENT CARDS

Job

Open the sealed Test window once for the validation survivors with frozen parameters, and issue a deployment card for every unit that passes.

Steps

  1. Check the LOCK (B.20) and write the hash of the unit set being opened.
  2. Load the full history; simulate each unit on Test with frozen parameters.
  3. Stationary bootstrap (10,000 draws): mean and 95% CI.
  4. DSR on the Test series with N = number of P6 units (PSR against 0 when N = 1).
  5. Cost × 1.5 on Test.
  6. Deployment card per passing unit: instrument, trigger, filter, side, T3 length and vf, ATR length, SL_ATR, TP_R, max bars, round-trip cost, stop slippage, Test CI.
Gates — per unit (hard, all required)
  1. g_trades: ≥ 30 Test trades.
  2. g_ci_lo: 95% CI lower bound of the Test mean > 0.
  3. g_dsr: DSR ≥ 0.95.
  4. g_cost: mean > 0 at cost × 1.5.
  5. g_sealed: the Test window was not opened before for a different unit set.
Gate — phase: ≥ 1 unit passes.
Outputs — test.csv, deployment_cards.csv, LOCK; test.png (whole-history equity with Validation and Test shaded; Test CI forest).
Closeout — Forwards the deployment cards. python run.py P6

P7 — PINE PARITY, INCUBATION & KILL SWITCH

Job

Reproduce each deployment card in TradingView with T3_Entry_Lab_v2.pine, prove parity with the engine, then let real time decide.

Steps

  1. Chart: the instrument's continuous contract, 30-minute bars, full electronic session.
  2. Card: Trigger, Filter, Side, T3 length, T3 volume factor, ATR length, Stop loss (× ATR), Take profit (× stop), Max bars in trade.
  3. Costs: Properties → commission per contract and slippage ticks equal to the card's costs.
  4. Parity replay: "Trade from / Trade until" = the Test dates of P0/splits.csv; compare the strategy table (trades, win rate, net mean R, MFE / MAE, bars held, exits SL / TP / TIME / EOS) with the unit's row of P6/test.csv.
  5. Incubation: paper or simulated trading from today until ≥ 30 trades.
  6. Kill switch, checked after every trade from the 30th: stop when the live mean RATR is below the Test CI lower bound, or the live drawdown exceeds the worst drawdown of the unit's Dev + Validation + Test history.
  7. A killed unit is never restarted with new parameters; new parameters are a new program from P2 (B.20).
Gates (hard)
  1. Parity: Pine trade count within ±5% of the engine's Test trade count, and Pine net mean R inside the unit's Test 95% CI (the engine run with intrabar: tv gives the exact TradingView fill rule).
  2. Incubation: after ≥ 30 trades, live mean RATR ≥ Test CI lower bound and drawdown within the historical worst.
Outputs — parity screenshot per card; live trade log (RATR, MFE / MAE, exit type); kill-switch record.
Closeout — A unit that passes incubation goes live; a unit that trips the kill switch is closed for good.
