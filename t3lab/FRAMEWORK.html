T3 EDGE LAB — BUILD FRAMEWORK

10,046 pre-registered candidates in 21 families, run on five futures markets through five stages. The first pool keeps every earlier candidate (5,046). The second pool adds 5,000 new candidates built on new session-anchored entry logic, with the T3 engine as their trend context. Stage I proves that an edge exists before any cost is applied. To pass, it must beat the drift of simply being in the market at that time of day, survive multiple testing at the family and member level, and beat its own luck in every randomization test, 999 draws each, with the selection of the best member reproduced inside the randomization. It must also hold on many out-of-sample paths after deflation by every trial ever run on these data. Only certified edges move on: they meet their costs on the micro and the mini contract (Stage II), receive exits chosen walk-forward (Stage III), face the Validation and the sealed Test windows once each (Stage IV), and go live under a kill switch (Stage V). A stage that ends with no survivor ends the program, and that is a valid result.

THE LOCKED PILLARS

1LUCK IS THE DEFAULT HYPOTHESIS

Every result is assumed to be luck until it beats its own luck distribution in each randomization test of B.13. Those tests reshuffle the same prices, place the same trades at random times and shift the same signals in time. The selection of the best of many candidates is repeated inside every randomization.

2EDGE BEFORE COSTS, PROVEN FIRST

Stage I works on gross results only and asks one question: is there a real timing edge? Costs cannot create an edge, only remove one. No candidate sees its costs before it holds an Edge Certificate.

3NO LEAKAGE, NO PEEKING

Every rule reads only bars up to and including t. The universe, the configuration and every threshold are frozen and hashed before any statistic exists (F2). Validation is opened once (P8) and Test once (P9). Every candidate whose idea was already measured on Dev carries the ★ flag and faces stricter exams.

4EVERY TRIAL COUNTED, FOREVER

Every unit, every parameter set and every earlier program on the same Dev data enters the trial ledger. Their effective number drives every Deflated Sharpe Ratio.

5CONSERVATIVE EXECUTION UNDER TOPSTEP RULES

The signal is taken at the bar close and filled at the next open. When stop and target fall in the same bar, the stop wins, and a gap fills at the open. Every position is flat by 15:08 CT. No position is held across a session end or a contract roll.

6BOTH CONTRACTS, REAL COSTS

Every certified edge is priced for both the micro and the mini contract with TopstepX fees and slippage. A contract is deployed only where its net result is significant and its dollar risk fits the account.

7PRICE DIFFERENCES IN ATR, NEVER PERCENT

The files are back-adjusted by addition, and CL even has negative prices. Every rule, stop, target and result therefore uses price differences measured in ATR or ticks. Ratios and percentages of price are never used.

A.2 — The program in plain words

  1. F0–F2. Clean the data and lock away the last 40% of the history. Prove that the machine and every test work: on fake markets with no edge, the tests must almost never certify anything, and on fake markets with a planted edge, they must find it. Then write down and freeze all 10,046 candidates.
  2. P1. For every candidate on every market, look at what price did after the signal compared with any ordinary moment at the same time of day. Nothing is selected yet.
  3. P2. Ask each family as a whole whether it has an edge on a market, one test per family, so that thousands of weak members cannot hide a real family.
  4. P3. Inside the families that passed, find the members that carry the edge, with a correction for having picked those families.
  5. P4. Try to destroy every survivor. Shuffle the market 999 times and rerun the whole selection each time, place the same trades at random times 999 times, and shift the signals in time 999 times. Add noise to prices, remove drift, change neighbouring settings, split by years and regimes, and test many out-of-sample paths. Finally deflate by every trial ever run. A survivor of all of this is certified as not luck.
  6. P5. Only now apply costs, for micro and for mini, and keep only edges that pay their costs at least twice over.
  7. P6–P7. Choose stops, targets and holding time on past blocks only, then prove again that the net result is not luck and that the risk fits the Topstep account.
  8. P8–P9. Open the locked Validation years once, then the locked Test years once.
  9. P10. Rebuild it in Pine, check that it matches, trade it in simulation for 30 trades, and stop it at once if it breaks.

A.3 — The gate law

  1. Every gate is a fixed rule whose fixed threshold is written into the configuration before F2 freezes the universe.
  2. Unit gates are columns g_*, and a unit continues only if all its hard gates are true. Report figures are printed but decide nothing.
  3. A phase closes PASS when at least one unit continues and STOP otherwise. The next phase refuses to start after a STOP.
  4. Selection and testing never use the same statistic twice on the same data. P1 measures without selecting, P2 selects families, P3 selects members, and P4 attacks the members with tests that P2 and P3 did not use.

PART B — STANDING LAWS

B.1 — Never changes

  1. Raw files are never modified. Repairs happen on the loaded copy and are counted.
  2. The configuration, the universe and every threshold are frozen at F2. Any later change opens a new program (B.20).
  3. No gate is loosened after a result is seen, and no candidate is added because of a result.
  4. Validation and Test results never change any choice.
  5. Nothing is ranked on win rate, profit factor or in-sample equity.
  6. A single grid peak is never chosen. Plateaus and neighbours decide (B.16, B.18).

B.2 — The data law

  1. The input is one 30-minute file per market with timestamp, open, high, low, close, volume, contract in CT. The prices are continuous and back-adjusted by addition.
  2. Back-adjustment by addition keeps price differences and destroys price ratios. All rules and results therefore use differences in ATR or ticks (Pillar 7).
  3. A roll is a change in the contract column. No trade, rule window or session anchor mixes two contracts without the adjustment, and no position crosses a roll.
  4. Timestamps are bar open times in CT local time (America/Chicago, with daylight saving), so every clock rule follows the exchange in summer and in winter.
  5. Holidays and early closes. A session has the bars it has and ends at its last bar on the exchange calendar. A missing OB or SB leaves that session's anchors empty: R_prev, H_prev and L_prev need both, S_prev needs SB, and a rule that reads an empty anchor is false. Slot histories (στ, the SLOT z) only take the slots a session has.

B.3 — The execution law

  1. The signal comes from bars up to t at the close of bar t. The market entry is at the open of bar t + 1, or later in the delay test.
  2. There is no entry when bar t + 1 is in another session or another contract, outside the window, after the last tradable bar, or when ATR[t] ≤ 0.
  3. One position at a time. A new signal on the exit bar is allowed.
  4. Shorts run on mirrored prices: (o, h, l, c) → (−o, −l, −h, −c). Every line built from price (T3, VWAP, session levels, στ) is mirrored, and volume is unchanged.
  5. Sessions: the CME day starts at 17:00 CT (session date = timestamp + 7 h), and a gap of 120 minutes or more also starts a new session.
  6. Flat by 15:08 CT (TopstepX). A bar that closes after 15:08 and opens before 17:00 cannot hold a position. The last tradable bar is known at its own close: it is the bar whose next scheduled bar would close after 15:08, or the session's last bar on the exchange calendar. No entry is made from it, and open trades close at its close. If that bar is missing from the data, the first bar after it closes the trade at its close, as TradingView does.
Basis: next-bar execution [R10]; CME Globex trading day [R22]; Topstep flat by 15:10 CT with the platform flattening from 15:08 CT [R23].

B.9 — The leakage law

  1. Causality. Every trigger and context reads only bars up to t. The acceptance test perturbs every bar after t and requires identical signals up to t (F1).
  2. Session anchors. OB, S_prev, H_prev, L_prev, R_prev, στ, VWAP and the volatility terciles use completed bars only. A value exists only from the bar at which it is known.
  3. Sealing. Test rows are hashed in F0 and dropped from every load before P9. Validation rows are not touched before P8.
  4. Pre-registration. The universe, the configuration, this framework and every threshold are hashed in F2 before any Stage I statistic exists.
  5. The ★ flag. A candidate whose idea was already measured on Dev by an earlier program carries ★. This covers every Pool L candidate and the Pool N families OPENING_RANGE, INTRADAY_MOMENTUM, T3_DAY_TREND and EXTREME_REVERSION. A ★ candidate passes every Dev gate unchanged and then faces stricter exams (P8).
  6. No cross-window figures. Normalisers (terciles, medians, στ, drift baselines) are computed causally or within the window in use. Validation and Test never feed a Dev figure.
Basis: [R10, R12, R20].

B.10 — The overfitting law

  1. No parameter is fitted in Stage I. The universe is fixed, and only the selection is a search, which P4 reproduces inside every randomization.
  2. Exits and T3 settings are chosen only in P6, inside walk-forward training blocks, by a plateau rule.
  3. Every selection is followed by a test on data the selection did not use: CPCV, walk-forward, Validation, Test.
  4. Every search is measured: PBO for the universe (P1 report), each selected family (P4) and the exit search (P6–P7).
  5. Every deciding Sharpe ratio is deflated by the ledger N (B.11).
  6. Robustness is asked of the idea, not of one setting: neighbours, price noise, horizons, sub-periods and regimes.

B.17 — The calibration and power law

  1. False certificates. Before any real result exists, Stage I (P1–P4, with every gate) runs on 100 null markets per instrument built by N1 from that instrument's Dev data. It uses a stratified 10% sample of the universe: 1,005 candidates covering every family in proportion. The number of null markets that yield at least one Edge Certificate is the measured false-certificate count.
  2. Power. The same Stage I runs on null markets in which a known member carries a planted edge: an extra drift spread evenly over the bars of its screen hold after each of its signals. The sizes are 0.10, 0.20 and 0.30 ATR per trade, with 20 runs per size and the instruments taken in rotation. Inside the power runs, the randomization tests use B = 199.
  3. Minimum detectable edge (MDE). The MDE is the smallest planted size that is certified in at least 80% of the runs. P5 compares it with each contract's cost per trade in ATR.
Basis: size and power by simulation [R12, R32, R33].

B.18 — The exit search law

  1. The objective is the 25th percentile, over the training blocks, of each block's mean net RATR per session. Fewer than 30 training trades scores −∞.
  2. Stage A applies only to members that use T3: the T3 grid L × v (72 points) with the screen exit. A setting is allowed only if its Dev entries keep a Jaccard overlap of at least 0.70 with the certified member's entries.
  3. Stage B: SL_ATR {0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0} × TP_R {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, none} × max bars {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, flat-by} = 216 points.
  4. Plateau rule: each score is replaced by the mean of the finite scores in its 3 × 3 (× 3) neighbourhood, and the best smoothed score wins.
Basis: [R3, R10, R20].

B.19 — The closeout law

Each phase writes a report (what it did, what was tried, what was changed, what was found, and the gates), together with its tables, its plots and a gates file reading PASS or STOP. The report is read before the next phase starts.

B.20 — The amendment and seal law

  1. Any change to the data, rules, universe, configuration or gates after F2 opens a new program with a new output folder. The old program's trials stay in the ledger.
  2. P9 writes a lock holding the hash of the strategies it opened the Test window for. A later P9 on a different set is refused, or is marked contaminated and fails.

B.21 — The verification law

  1. The fast code must equal slow reference implementations written from this framework: the engine, both drift baselines, every trigger and context of both pools, T3, ATR, VWAP, στ and the session anchors. The Pool N reference is streamed bar by bar with the same state and order of updates as T3_Edge_Lab.pine.
  2. Shorts mirror longs exactly, and no rule reads future bars.
  3. The statistics must equal independent references: statsmodels for HAC and BH; Monte Carlo for E[max SR], the null SR variance and the DSR false-positive rate; known inputs for PBO, CPCV, the bootstrap, SPA, StepM, Simes, Benjamini–Bogomolov and Keff.
  4. Each null of B.13 returns uniform p-values on null markets (Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, 200 markets).
  5. B.17 calibration and power are measured and reported.
  6. Second engines: an independent implementation of B.3–B.4 on another framework (Backtrader) reproduces every trade of the engine, both sides, with stops, targets, time exits and session ends; vectorbt reproduces every Stage I screen trade.
  7. Property tests (Hypothesis, thousands of random small markets): the engine equals its slow reference; every entry is at the next open inside the session; one position at a time; a stop never fills better than the stop, a target never worse than the target.
  8. Holiday markets: the Pool N reference, MARKET_AGREE and the session ends of item 1 also hold on synthetic markets with early closes, missing OB and SB bars, a halt inside a session and missing sessions.
Basis: property-based testing [R52].

Job

  4. Per market, compute: Keff of the new series (ledger); PBO of picking the Dev winner; Hansen SPA p.
Gate (hard): ≥ 100 Dev trades. Units below the floor are recorded as insufficient and leave.
Outputs: units table, universe table, ledger update. Plots: t distribution against N(0,1) per market; family × market heatmap of the mean t; excess by hour per family; horizon profiles per family.

P2 — FAMILY TEST (DOES THE FAMILY HAVE AN EDGE?)

Job

Ask first whether a family has an edge on a market at all, with one test per family, side and market, so that a real family is not drowned by thousands of members.

Plain words: before judging single candidates, ask each family as a team whether it beats chance.

Steps

  1. For each of the 210 family tests, compute the Simes p over the members' P1 p-values, the pooled p of the averaged member series, and the family p (A.6).
  2. Apply BH across the 210 family p-values at q = 0.05. The selected family tests number R.
  3. Report Stouffer z across markets per family and side, the share of members with positive excess, and the pooled mean excess.
Gate (hard): R ≥ 1.
Outputs: family table (Simes p, pooled p, family p, q). Plot: family × side × market map of q.
Basis: [R5, R30, R31].

P3 — MEMBER SELECTION (WHICH MEMBERS CARRY IT?)

Job

Inside the selected family tests only, find the members that carry the edge, with a correction that accounts for the family selection.

Plain words: inside the winning teams, find the players who actually score, with stricter rules because the teams were picked first.

Steps

  1. Within each selected family test, apply BH at level 0.10 × R / 210 over its members (Benjamini–Bogomolov).
  2. Report Romano-Wolf StepM on the same members (family-wise 10%, 999 joint bootstrap draws).
  3. Keep members with all of: a discovery; excess > 0; edge ratio > 1; positive excess in ≥ 4 of 6 Dev blocks.
  4. De-duplicate within each market: sort by t, and drop a member whose entries overlap a kept member by Jaccard > 0.30.
  5. Keep at most 50 members per market, highest t first.
Gates (hard, per member)
  1. g_bb: Benjamini–Bogomolov discovery.
  2. g_excess: mean excess > 0.
  3. g_e_ratio: edge ratio > 1.
  4. g_blocks: ≥ 4 of 6 Dev blocks positive.
Gate (phase): ≥ 1 member.
Outputs: member table (BB q, StepM, block profile), dedupe log.
Basis: [R31, R32].

P4 — LUCK ELIMINATION & EDGE CERTIFICATE

Job

Try to destroy every surviving member with tests that P2–P3 did not use. A member that survives everything receives an Edge Certificate: evidence of a gross timing edge that is not luck, not drift, not one period, not one setting and not one path.

Plain words: make luck compete with the member 999 times in three different ways, including luck that also gets to pick the best of the family. Then shake the data, the settings and the years. Only a member that wins every time is certified.

Steps

  1. Selection-aware MCPT (N1-S, 999) once per selected family test. Compare each member's real t with the best-of-family t of every permuted market.
  2. Own MCPT (N1, 999), report only. Comparing it with N1-S shows how much of the significance came from selection.
  3. Random entry (N2, 999) and circular shift (N3, 999).
  4. Bootstrap CI (N4, 2,000) of the mean per-session excess.
  5. Detrended data (N5): rerun the member on detrended prices.
  6. Horizon coherence and delay profile (B.16).
  7. Price-noise perturbation: 100 copies (B.16).
  8. Neighbours (B.16).
  9. Stability (B.15) and concentration (B.16).
  10. CPCV of the family selection. In each of the 45 splits, pick the member with the highest t on the 8 training groups and record its excess on the 2 test groups, then assemble 9 paths. Also compute the PBO of the family's members (CSCV, 16 blocks).
  11. Gross PSR and DSR with the ledger N (B.11), and MinTRL against the number of Validation sessions.
  12. Report: clock placebo (B.16), breadth (the markets where the same candidate has excess > 0 and t ≥ 1), and SPA.
Gates (hard, all required): the Edge Certificate
  1. g_mcpt_sel: selection-aware MCPT p ≤ 0.01, beating at least 990 of 999 best-of-family luck draws.
  2. g_random: random-entry p ≤ 0.01.
  3. g_shift: circular-shift p ≤ 0.01.
  4. g_ci: 95% bootstrap CI lower bound of the excess > 0.
  5. g_detrend: excess on detrended data > 0 with t ≥ 2.
  6. g_horizon: excess > 0 at the neighbouring horizons that exist.
  7. g_noise: t ≥ 2 in ≥ 90 of 100 perturbed copies.
  8. g_neighbours: ≥ 70% of neighbours with positive excess, and their median > 0.
  9. g_blocks5: ≥ 5 of 6 Dev blocks positive.
  10. g_years: ≥ 70% of years positive, and the total positive without the best year.
  11. g_stable: no judged bucket significantly negative.
  12. g_trim: excess > 0 without the best 5% of trades.
  13. g_cpcv: ≥ 7 of 9 CPCV paths positive.
  14. g_pbo: PBO of the family's members ≤ 0.20.
  15. g_dsr: gross DSR ≥ 0.95.
  16. g_power: MinTRL ≤ Validation sessions.
Gate (phase): ≥ 1 Edge Certificate.
Outputs: certificate table (every test's value and gate per member, with ★). Plots: null histograms with the real value marked (N1-S, N1, N2, N3); CPCV paths; horizon and delay profiles; noise distribution; neighbour map; stability bars; clock placebo; gate matrix.
Closeout: Only certified members enter Stage II. A STOP here means no family has a gross timing edge on these markets at this bar size that survives luck.
Basis: [R1, R2, R3, R9, R10, R11, R12, R20, R33, R43].
STAGE II — COSTS (micro and mini)

P5 — COST HURDLE

Job

Price every certified edge with both contracts, and keep the pairs in which the edge pays its cost with a margin.

Plain words: the edge is real; now see whether it is big enough to pay the broker and slippage on the micro and on the mini.

Steps

  1. For each certified member and each permitted contract (micro, mini): the cost per trade in ATR (B.5), the net result per trade, the per-session net series, and its Newey-West t.
  2. The break-even cost multiple: BCM = gross excess ÷ cost.
  3. Stresses: cost × 1.5 and × 2; +1 tick per side.
  4. The cost per trade compared with the instrument's MDE (F1).
Gates (hard, per member × contract)
  1. g_net: net mean > 0.
  2. g_t_net: net Newey-West t ≥ 2.
  3. g_bcm: BCM ≥ 2.
  4. g_cost15: net mean > 0 at cost × 1.5.
  5. g_slip: net mean > 0 with +1 tick per side.
Report: cost × 2, cost against MDE. Gate (phase): ≥ 1 member × contract pair.
Outputs: cost table per pair. Plots: gross excess against cost per trade (micro and mini); BCM bars.
Basis: [R22, R23, R34].
STAGE III — CONSTRUCTION

P6 — EXITS & PARAMETERS (WALK-FORWARD)

Job

Choose the T3 setting, stop, target and hold for each member × contract without scoring any choice on the data it was chosen on.

Plain words: pick the stop, target and holding time using only older years, then check them on the next year, and repeat.

Steps

  1. Folds k = 1…5: train on B0…B(k−1) and score on Bk.
  2. On the training blocks, run Stage A (members using T3, with the Jaccard ≥ 0.70 rule) and then Stage B (B.18), apply the plateau rule, and apply the result unchanged to Bk.
  3. Stitch B1…B5 into the walk-forward out-of-sample (WF-OOS) net series.
  4. Final parameters: the same procedure on B0…B5.
  5. Ledger: the configurations evaluated, the Keff of those configurations, and the PBO of the search.
Gates (hard): WF-OOS trades ≥ 50; WF-OOS net mean > 0.
Outputs: refined table, fold table. Plots: exit surfaces (SL × TP at the chosen hold), fold results.

P7 — NET EVIDENCE & ACCOUNT FIT

Job

Show that the constructed strategy keeps its edge after costs, the exit search, stress and time, and that one contract fits the Topstep account.

Plain words: with the real exits and real costs, prove again that it is not luck, and that one bad stretch cannot blow the account.

Steps

  1. WF-OOS net: stationary bootstrap CI (2,000); PSR; net DSR with N = ledger N + Keff(configurations); MinTRL.
  2. Random-entry and circular-shift nulls on net R with the final parameters (999 each).
  3. PBO of the exit search.
  4. Stress (cost × 1.5, +1 tick, one-bar delay, stop fills 2 ticks worse), neighbours, concentration, stability.
  5. Account fit for one contract: stop risk = SL_ATR × median Dev ATR × point value; the worst WF-OOS drawdown in dollars.
  6. Report a Combine simulation: stationary-bootstrap paths of the WF-OOS daily net dollar P&L under the account's trailing maximum loss limit, giving the share of paths that reach the profit target before the limit, per contract.
Gates (hard)
  1. CI lower bound > 0; net DSR ≥ 0.95; both nulls p ≤ 0.05; PBO of the exit search ≤ 0.30.
  2. Positive at cost × 1.5, at +1 tick, with a one-bar delay and with stop fills 2 ticks worse; neighbours' median > 0; positive without the top 5% of trades and without the best year; no significantly negative bucket.
  3. MinTRL ≤ Validation sessions.
  4. Stop risk ≤ the configured maximum loss per trade; worst drawdown ≤ the configured drawdown budget.
Outputs: evidence table per strategy (member × contract). Plots: WF-OOS equity, CI forest, null histograms, Combine simulation, gate matrix.
STAGE IV — EXAMS (one look each)

P8 — VALIDATION

Job

Run each P7 strategy once on Validation with frozen parameters. The edge must still exist before costs and must still pay after costs.

Plain words: first exam, on years the program has never seen. One try, no changes.

Steps

  1. Per strategy: gross excess (drift baselines built within Validation by bar time) and net result.
  2. Stationary bootstrap (10,000), with BH across strategies.
  3. Random-entry null on gross R (999).
  4. Cost × 1.5; MinTRL of the Validation net series against the number of Test sessions.
  5. Refutation test: build the comparison the family names ("refuted if", C.2 and C.7) with the strategy's own side, hold and exits, and run it once on Validation.
Gates (hard)
  1. Trades ≥ 30; gross excess > 0; net mean > 0.
  2. Not ★: BH q ≤ 0.05 and random-entry p ≤ 0.05. ★: BH q ≤ 0.01 and random-entry p ≤ 0.01.
  3. Net > 0 at cost × 1.5; MinTRL ≤ Test sessions.
  4. Not refuted: the mean gross excess per trade is above the comparison's.
Outputs: validation table. Plots: equity through Dev and Validation, CI forest.

P9 — SEALED TEST

Job

Open the Test window once for the Validation survivors, and issue deployment cards.

Plain words: final exam on the most recent years, which were locked from the start.

Steps

  1. Check the lock and write it (B.20).
  2. Run the frozen strategies on Test: net per session, 95% CI (10,000), DSR with N = strategies tested, cost × 1.5, gross excess.
  3. Write a deployment card for each passing strategy: market, contract, family, trigger, contexts or filter, side, T3 setting, SL_ATR, TP_R, max bars, costs, flat-by, Test CI, worst drawdown, ★.
Gates (hard): trades ≥ 30; CI lower bound > 0; DSR ≥ 0.95; net > 0 at cost × 1.5; gross excess > 0; not contaminated.
Outputs: test table, deployment cards, lock. Plot: equity over the whole history with the windows shaded.

P10 — PINE PARITY, INCUBATION & KILL SWITCH

Job

Reproduce each card in TradingView, incubate it, and stop it at once if it breaks.

Steps

  1. Load the card into T3_Edge_Lab.pine on the instrument's continuous contract, with 30-minute bars, the electronic session and flat-by at 15:08 CT. Run the Pine Profiler on the full chart: the script must finish inside TradingView's execution-time limit (20 s on a basic plan, 40 s on the others).
  2. Run a parity replay on the Test dates, holiday sessions included, with commission 0 and slippage 0 against the engine with the TradingView intrabar path (B.4, option pessimistic = False): every entry and exit bar, price and exit type must be equal. Then set the card's commission per side and slippage.
  3. Incubate in simulation until ≥ 30 trades.
  4. Repaint check: every week of incubation, replay the same bars in TradingView's bar replay. Every live signal and fill must be identical: nothing appears, moves or disappears after its bar has closed (this also covers MARKET_AGREE, whose paired bar must be complete when the chart bar closes).
