## 1. (a) Quantower: range bars, tick bars, timing, volume analysis, the forming bar

### 1.1 Range bars — the official definition

Help page "Range bars" (VERIFIED — https://help.quantower.com/analytics-panels/chart/chart-types/range-bars; source https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Quantower/QuantowerKB/master/analytics-panels/chart/chart-types/range-bars.md), full rule text:

> To built range bars there are three simple rules:
> * Each range bar must have a high/low range that equals the specified range.
> * Every new range bar must open outside the high/low range of the previous bar.
> * Each range bar must close at either its high or its low.

and: "Range Bars does not depend on time, but only on price movement. Thus, if the price has low volatility and stays in a range that is less than specified in the parameters, then new bars (candles) will not be built."

The API help defines the parameter: `new HistoryAggregationRangeBars(int rangeBars)` — "rangeBars - the height (in ticks) of each bar". VERIFIED — https://help.quantower.com/quantower-algo/access-to-advanced-aggregations (raw: .../QuantowerKB/master/quantower-algo/access-to-advanced-aggregations.md). Bar height in price = `RangeBars * Symbol.TickSize`; the user's `DetectGeometry()` computes exactly this (INFERRED, TNXCVDPro.cs lines 1402–1408).

Consequences of the three rules on a finished bar: `High − Low == RangeBars × TickSize`, `Close ∈ {High, Low}`, and `Open(next) ∉ [Low, High]` of the previous bar. On the forming bar `High − Low < RangeBars × TickSize`. (Arithmetic from the VERIFIED rules.)

### 1.2 Gaps and "phantom" bars: not documented; no "range bars with gaps" variant exists

- The help's chart-type list has ten types: Tick chart, Time, Heikin-Ashi, Range Bars, Renko, Line Break, Kagi, Points & Figures, Volume, Reversal. No "range bars with gaps" type. VERIFIED — https://help.quantower.com/analytics-panels/chart/chart-types (raw .../chart-types/README.md).
- `HistoryAggregationRangeBars` has exactly two settings: `SettingItemInteger("Range bars", RangeBars) { Minimum = 1, Increment = 1 }` and a history-type selector. There is no gap/no-gap option, and the aggregation name constant is the single string `SETTINGS_AGGREGATION_RANGE_BARS = "Range bars"`. DECOMPILED — DEC_HistoryAggregationRangeBars.cs.
- The help text says nothing about what happens when one trade prints more than one range away from the previous bar. If the three rules are applied literally, such a jump can only satisfy "range equals the specified range" and "open outside the previous range" by inserting intermediate bars, or by letting the new bar's open sit far from the previous close (a visible gap). Which one Quantower does is UNVERIFIED (the range-bar processor is not in the decompiled mirror; the mirror only contains the BusinessLayer, and the builder classes live elsewhere).
- Other vendors document both behaviours, which is why the guard matters: CQG — "in case of significant price move there could be price gaps between bars" and a fast move "might show a long trend of several bars going upwards/downwards from the previous close" (SNIPPET — https://help.cqg.com/cqgic/25/Documents/rangebarrb.htm); TradeStation — "when there is a price gap in the underlying data, 'virtual bars' will be inserted as necessary to fill in the gap on the Range Bar chart", with zero up/down volume (SNIPPET — https://help.tradestation.com/10_00/eng/tradestationhelp/charting/rangebar_chart.htm).
- Design guard (my inference): treat a bar with `Volume == 0`, `Ticks == 0`, `VolumeAnalysisData == null`, `Total.Volume == 0`, or `TicksRight <= TicksLeft` as a possible phantom; never divide by its volume or duration; do not count it as a "fast" bar; and do not assume `High − Low == RangeBars × TickSize` holds on every historical bar (measure it, as the user's `RangeFull()` already does from the last 21 finished bars, TNXCVDPro.cs lines 1905–1916, INFERRED).

### 1.3 How range bars are built (data path)

- `HistoryAggregationRangeBars.GetAggregationToDirectDownload(metadata)` returns `new HistoryAggregationTick(historyType)` (with `Bid`/`Ask` mapped to `BidAsk`), or `null` when the connection cannot supply ticks of that type. `GetPeriod => Period.TICK1`. DECOMPILED — DEC_HistoryAggregationRangeBars.cs. So range bars are always built client-side from tick history; they are never downloaded pre-built. (01_quantower_api.md §3 says the same.)
- `BarCreationBehavior` is not overridden → base value `Realtime` (base: `public virtual BarCreationBehavior BarCreationBehavior => BarCreationBehavior.Realtime;`). Renko overrides it to `Deferred`. DECOMPILED — HistoryAggregation.cs line 73, HistoryAggregationRenko.cs. Meaning: the forming range bar exists as item [0] and is updated tick by tick, unlike Renko where bricks appear only when complete (Renko help: "Build current bar — is the option that shows/visualize the creation of the current brick in real-time", VERIFIED .../chart-types/renko.md).
- `IsWaitingFirstQuoteRequired` is not overridden on range bars (false) but is `true` on tick bars. DECOMPILED. What the flag changes is UNVERIFIED.
- History types offered in the range-bar settings selector: values 3, 5, 0, 1, 2 = `Last, Mark, Bid, Ask, Midpoint` (enum order `{ Bid, Ask, Midpoint, Last, BidAsk, Mark }`, DEC HistoryType.cs). Tick bars add 4 = `BidAsk`. DECOMPILED. A range-bar chart built from Bid or Midpoint has no trade volume in its OHLC source; its per-bar volume analysis still comes from trades inside the bar's time window (see 1.6) — UNVERIFIED but the only consistent reading.
- `HistoryAggregationRangeBars.ToString()` renders as "Range bars: N t" style (name + RangeBars + short tick suffix). DECOMPILED. Chart tag text for the card can mimic this.

### 1.4 Tick bars

- Help "Tick Bars" (VERIFIED — https://help.quantower.com/analytics-panels/chart/chart-types/tick-chart, raw .../chart-types/tick-chart.md): "Tick Bars build a new bar after a set number of trades, for example after every 500 trades." "A tick can be a trade of just one contract, or 10,000 contracts. This is why it's crucial to combine the use of tick charts with volume." "Depending on the type of instrument (Futures, Crypto, or Forex/CFD), the user can select the required data type for constructing tick bars."
- Class: `public sealed class HistoryAggregationTickBars : HistoryAggregation, IHistoryAggregationHistoryTypeSupport`; `int TicksCount` (public get, private set), `HistoryType HistoryType`; `GetPeriod => new Period(BasePeriod.Tick, TicksCount)`; `DefaultRange => Period.HOUR1`; `IsWaitingFirstQuoteRequired => true`; settings: `SettingItemInteger("Ticks count", TicksCount) { Minimum = 1 }` + history type. DECOMPILED — DEC_HistoryAggregationTickBars.cs. `get_TicksCount` is in the DLL dump → exists in 1.146.7 (INFERRED).
- Direct download: if the provider lists "Tick bars" in `AllowedAggregations` the bars are downloaded as such (`return this`); otherwise built from `HistoryAggregationTick`. DECOMPILED. So unlike range bars, tick bars may come pre-built from the feed.
- `Symbol.GetHistory(Period, HistoryType, from, to)` maps `Period(Tick, n>1)` → `HistoryAggregationTickBars(n)` and `Period.TICK1` → `HistoryAggregationTick`. DECOMPILED — Symbol.cs line 2432.
- Distinct from `HistoryAggregationTick` ("Tick": one bar per tick). Official scripts test `Aggregation is HistoryAggregationTick` to special-case pure tick charts. VERIFIED — IndicatorSpeedOfTape.cs line 63, 103.
- `HistoryItemBar.Ticks` ("Defines ticks amount", `long`) is the number of ticks in the bar. DECOMPILED — HistoryItemBar.cs line 155–158; `Indicator.Ticks(offset)` in the DLL dump (INFERRED). On a finished tick bar it should equal `TicksCount`, and on the forming bar it is the progress counter ("25 of 40 trades" in the user's guide, INFERRED make_guide.py line 332). That equality is not stated anywhere official: UNVERIFIED.

### 1.5 Is bar duration exposed? Yes, for finished bars

- `HistoryItem.TicksLeft` / `TimeLeft` = left border (UTC). VERIFIED — KB downloading-history ("left border of the bar"), cited in 01 §3.
- `HistoryItemBar.TicksRight` / `TimeRight` — doc comment "Gets bar's right time border" (on `TimeRight`). DECOMPILED — HistoryItemBar.cs lines 44–60.
- Official use as duration for non-time aggregations, IndicatorSpeedOfTape.cs lines 66–72 (VERIFIED — https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Quantower/Scripts/master/Indicators/IndicatorSpeedOfTape.cs):
  ```csharp
  double seconds;
  if (this.HistoricalData.Aggregation is HistoryAggregationTime aggregationTime)
      seconds = aggregationTime.Period.Duration.TotalSeconds;
  else
      seconds = new TimeSpan(currHistoryItem.TicksRight - currHistoryItem.TicksLeft).TotalSeconds;
  double speed = number / seconds;
  ```
  The same script refuses to run on a pure tick chart (`Aggregation is HistoryAggregationTick`) because every bar has zero duration.
- Forming bar: when `HistoricalData` extends a tick-built history it inspects the last loaded item and, `if (historyItem2.TicksRight == 0L)`, drops it and rebuilds from its `TicksLeft`; otherwise it continues from `TicksRight + 1`. DECOMPILED — HistoricalData.cs lines 853–863 (the branch for `HistoryAggregationTickBars`). This is strong evidence that an unfinished bar carries `TicksRight == 0` at least in loaded history. Whether the live forming bar's `TicksRight` is 0, equals `TicksLeft`, or tracks the last tick is UNVERIFIED. Guard: for the forming bar use `now − TicksLeft` (with `Core.Instance.TimeUtils.DateTimeUtcNow`, VERIFIED in 01 §5) and treat `TicksRight <= TicksLeft` as "unknown" on finished bars.
- No "time to next bar" for range or tick bars: `IHistoryProcessor.GetTimeToNextBar()` returns `null` in the base processor and is not reachable from `Indicator`. DECOMPILED — HistoryProcessor.cs line 112.
- `Indicator.Time(offset)` returns `TimeLeft`; the chart places the bar at `GetChartX(TimeLeft)`. DECOMPILED + VERIFIED usage (01 §1.6, §3).

### 1.6 How volume analysis attaches to range and tick bars

- `VolumeAnalysisManager.CalculateProfile(HistoricalData, VolumeAnalysisCalculationParameters)` — doc comment "Calculate volume profile for each bar in History Data". The request spans `From = historicalData[0, Begin].TimeLeft` to `To = historicalData.ToTime` (or now). It picks one of two internal calculators by `historicalData.Aggregation.BarCreationBehavior` (`Deferred` → the Renko-style calculator; `Realtime` → the ordinary one). DECOMPILED — VolumeAnalysisManager.cs lines 57–80. Range bars and tick bars take the `Realtime` path like time bars. The per-bar slicing is done inside the calculator by the bar's time borders (that is the only per-bar information the request carries) — the exact rule (inclusive/exclusive `TicksRight`) is UNVERIFIED.
- Result: each `HistoryItemBar.VolumeAnalysisData` gets its own `PriceLevels` (price → `VolumeAnalysisItem`) and `Total`. Official scripts read it the same way on any aggregation (IndicatorDeltaDots.cs lines 155–171 iterate `currentData.VolumeAnalysisData.PriceLevels`; IndicatorSpeedOfTape.cs uses `VolumeAnalysisData` on everything but pure tick charts). VERIFIED. The user's indicator loads it on 8-tick range bars and 40-trade tick bars (INFERRED — guide line 300, `GetVolumeAnalysisData(...)?.Total` throughout TNXCVDPro.cs). The opt-in (`IVolumeAnalysisIndicator`, `VolumeAnalysisData_Loaded`, `VolumeAnalysisCalculationProgress.State == Finished`) is documented in 01 §2.
- Live updates: `HistoricalData.HistoryItemVolumeAnalysisUpdated` "fires when a bar's volume analysis changes" (VERIFIED — api docs extract quoted in 01 §1.7); the forming bar's `VolumeAnalysisData` is updated in real time (official DeltaDots/SpeedOfTape read `HistoricalData[Count-1].VolumeAnalysisData` live — VERIFIED usage).
- Per-level fields on `VolumeAnalysisItem` (DECOMPILED — scratchpad `decompiled_mirror/VolumeAnalysisItem.cs`): `Trades, BuyTrades, SellTrades, Volume, BuyVolume, SellVolume, FilteredTotalVolume, FilteredBuyVolume, FilteredSellVolume, MaxOneTradeVolume, MinDelta, MaxDelta, BuyVolumePercent, SellVolumePercent, Delta (= Buy − Sell), DeltaPercent, CumulativeDelta, FilteredTotalVolumePercent, FilteredBuyVolumePercent, FilteredSellVolumePercent, MaxOneTradeVolumePercent, AverageSize, AverageBuySize, AverageSellSize, DeltaFinish (= Delta − the larger-magnitude of MinDelta/MaxDelta), GetValue(VolumeAnalysisField)`. Of these, `BuyVolume, SellVolume, Volume, Delta, MaxDelta, MinDelta` are in the DLL dump → exist in 1.146.7 (INFERRED). The rest match the data types the cluster-chart help lists ("Trades", "Average size", "Max one trade volume (value and %)", "Filtered volume", VERIFIED .../cluster-chart.md), so they almost certainly exist, but their presence in 1.146.7 is UNVERIFIED until compiled against the stubs. `MaxOneTradeVolume` is the hook for a "large-lot participation" measure (section 3, rule 9).
- "Filtered volume" is a general volume-analysis setting: "Set the minimum volume values that will be displayed on the chart ... All values that are less than the filtered volume are presented as zeros." "Custom step summarizes the volume data of as many price levels as specified in the setting. By default, the value of the custom step is set to 1 tick." VERIFIED — cluster-chart.md. These two are the native "minimum volume" and "level aggregation" knobs; an indicator sees raw 1-tick levels unless it aggregates itself (UNVERIFIED whether the chart's custom step affects `PriceLevels` handed to indicators).

### 1.7 How the forming bar updates (what the indicator sees)

- `OnUpdate(UpdateArgs)` reasons: `HistoricalBar` during load, `NewTick` on each tick of the forming bar, `NewBar` when a bar starts. VERIFIED — KB simple-moving-average-example (quoted in 01 §1.2). On a new live bar the data layer updates `OnBarClose` indicators (reason `NewBar`), appends the new item, updates `OnTick` indicators, then fires `NewHistoryItem`; every later tick replaces item [0] and fires `HistoryItemUpdated`. DECOMPILED — HistoricalData.cs `AddNewItem` lines 1399–1417 (01 §1.2 has the same reading).
- `HistoryProcessorItemUpdatedEventArgs.ItemOffset` (default 0) exists on the processor event — the processor can report an update to a bar other than [0]. DECOMPILED — HistoryProcessorItemUpdatedEventArgs.cs. Whether range-bar building ever updates an earlier bar is UNVERIFIED; the safe pattern is the user's existing one: on every update re-read the last two bars, and on `NewBar` re-read from `min(_n, count) − 1` (TNXCVDPro.cs line 1499, INFERRED).
- Range-bar specific: one tick can finish a bar and open the next, and (if phantom bars exist, 1.2) could append several bars at once. Official idiom for "is this a new bar" on tick charts: `Aggregation.GetPeriod == Period.TICK1 ? args.Reason == UpdateReason.NewTick : args.Reason == UpdateReason.NewBar` (VERIFIED — IndicatorHighLow.cs; `GetPeriod` is Obsolete in the decompile, prefer type tests). On range bars `GetPeriod` is also `TICK1` (DECOMPILED), so that idiom would wrongly treat every tick as a new bar on a range chart — test `Aggregation is HistoryAggregationTick` instead, as SpeedOfTape does (VERIFIED).
- Progress of the forming bar: range bars `(High − Low) / (RangeBars × TickSize)`, tick bars `Ticks / TicksCount` (the user's "Bar now" row, INFERRED guide line 332). Both are readable from `HistoricalData[0]`.

### 1.8 Quantower's own imbalance (cluster chart "Imbalance" view)

Official help, VERIFIED — https://help.quantower.com/analytics-panels/chart/volume-analysis-tools/cluster-chart (raw .../volume-analysis-tools/cluster-chart.md):

> **Imbalance** in the footprint chart highlights the price levels where a buy trade volume is excess over a sell trade volume. Diagonal Bid/Ask imbalance displays aggressive buy market orders lifting the offer and aggressive sell market orders hitting the bid.

> In the Imbalance mode, by default, the **Sell volume (Bid)** is selected on the left side of the bar, and the **Buy volume (Ask)** on the right side. Other data types cannot be selected in this mode.

Also VERIFIED on that page: "Delta % = Delta / Volume * 100"; data types "Buy (or Sell) Volume, %", "Max one trade volume (value and %)", "Filtered volume", "Average size"; coloring modes "by delta, by volume, by trades, etc."; the ratio setting is reached via "the advanced settings (gear icon)". The markdown page does not contain the ratio value, a stacked-count option, or a per-mode minimum-volume option.

Quantower blog "Reading Stacked Imbalance on the Footprint Chart" (SNIPPET — https://www.quantower.com/blog/imbalance-footprint-chart-and-rithmic-plugin; page unreachable; quotes as returned by three separate searches):
- "Buy volume at the ask is measured against sell volume at the bid one level below, and sell volume at the bid against buy volume at the ask one level above."
- "Set it to 3 and Quantower highlights every level where one side traded more than 300% past the other. You can set several ratio levels at once and color each one."
- "most order-flow traders settle somewhere around 200–300%" (truncated in the snippet).
- "An unfinished auction — also called unfinished business — is a bar high or low that still shows volume on both the bid and the ask."
- "If price slices back through the stack, the aggression was genuine and momentum carries. If it stalls against the stack instead, you're usually seeing absorption" (the retest rule; see section 3).

So Quantower's definition is: diagonal, ratio-based (a multiple, not a percent-of-sum), several thresholds each with its own colour. Stacked-level count and a dedicated minimum-volume filter for the imbalance view: UNVERIFIED (not found in the help markdown or snippets; the general "Filtered volume" setting is the closest official knob). An ATAS-feedback post mentions "a stacked imbalance indicator on Quantower" whose filled imbalances disappear (SNIPPET — https://feedback.atas.net/p/stacked-imbalances-that-stay-when-unfilled-and-disappear-when-filled); that is a user's description, not documentation.

Official code idiom (VERIFIED — IndicatorDeltaDots.cs lines 173–199, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Quantower/Scripts/master/Indicators/IndicatorDeltaDots.cs): with the "diagonal comparison" option on, for each price level the script takes `sellValue = max(SellVolume at levels j = −1, 0, +1)` (the maximum of the level itself and both neighbours — a looser rule than the strict one-level-below pairing in the blog); a zero side becomes 1 unless `allowZeroComparsion`; the level is skipped when `item.Value.Volume <= minimumVolume` (unless `showSmallerVolume`) or when both `sellValue <= compareTreshold && buyValue <= compareTreshold`. This is Quantower's own guard set against zero division and small numbers, and it is safe to reuse.

### 2.1 Sierra Chart — Numbers Bars

### 2.8 Market-profile vocabulary (initiative vs responsive)
Dalton, "Mind over Markets" (book not read; summaries SNIPPET — https://completetradersedge.com/?p=230553, https://9natree.castos.com/episodes/review-mind-over-markets-power-trading-with-market-generated-information-james-f-dalton-summa): "Dalton separates initiating activity, which breaks into new territory, from responsive activity, which reacts within an already-established range"; "Balance is when the market is building value, rotating within a range, and rewarding responsive activity. Imbalance is when the market is discovering new value, trending, and rewarding initiative activity." In footprint use: a buy imbalance printed while price breaks above prior value is initiative; a sell imbalance printed into a prior high is responsive (my mapping of the vocabulary; UNVERIFIED as a vendor rule).

## 3. (c) What practitioners watch to call an imbalance "real" or "fake"

Every item is a practitioner heuristic. No source offered a backtest; two searches explicitly found none ("I found no backtested evidence for any of these rules"; "found no empirical study testing whether bar duration or absorption patterns on range bars predict price"). The academic record in 02 §j says the predictive horizon of trade imbalance is seconds to about two price changes, so these rules describe the bar that just happened and who is in control, not a forecast.

1. **Stacking, not a single level.** "A single imbalance is often noise. A cluster of three is rarely random. The more consecutive imbalances, the higher the probability of continuation." (ATAS Learn, SNIPPET.) LuxAlgo: "isolated ones are mostly noise; stacked imbalances across consecutive prices show initiative worth respecting." Practitioner heuristic.
2. **The retest is the test.** "If price slices back through the stack, the aggression was genuine and momentum carries. If it stalls against the stack instead, you're usually seeing absorption" (Quantower blog, SNIPPET). Same idea, as an explicit rule set: entry after three or more stacked levels at a session high/low "where the next bar fails to extend past that level"; invalidated "if a following bar pushes through the imbalanced level with matching volume and delta" — https://daytradingtoolkit.com/strategies/footprint-chart-day-trading-strategy (one vendor's framework). Practitioner heuristic. For an indicator this means the verdict on bar i is only final after bar i+1 (or i+k) is known; the forming-bar reading must be labelled provisional.
3. **Where in the bar the stack sits.** Stack at the extreme in the bar's direction + the next bar fails = exhaustion/trap; stack in the body with a close beyond the prior bar = continuation. ATAS "P" pattern: "heavy buying at the top of a candle that fails to push the price higher, exposing a strong passive seller wall" (SNIPPET). X-FP Imbalances: "consecutive buy stacks immediately followed by sell stacks in a tight range" = "buyer exhaustion and a climax reversal"; "a one-sided sell stack at a high, followed by price turning down, as a supply zone" — https://www.tradingview.com/script/1qgmYp7S-X-FP-Imbalances (SNIPPET). Tradovate forum: "a large positive imbalance after an extended move, followed by price failing to continue ... buyers as possibly trapped ... a move back through that area can fuel a reversal" — https://community.tradovate.com/t/simplified-orderflow-signals-trapped-trader-fuel-continuation/12710. The aggressor-flow-lib Pine library encodes it: "Continuation (4/5) -- stacked imbalances with strong delta, a close near the bar's extreme and a close beyond the prior bar"; "Exhaustion (2/3) -- a new swing extreme printed on a thin extreme row, with delta fading against the previous bars" — https://kr.tradingview.com/script/547WWgzV-aggressor-flow-lib/. Practitioner heuristics.
4. **Imbalance against the bar's direction = responsive side showing up.** Sell imbalances inside an up bar (or buy imbalances inside a down bar) are read as the passive/responsive side absorbing: "repeated buy imbalances that fail to lift price imply passive sellers are soaking up the aggression" (LuxAlgo); Jigsaw: "delta will continue to increase but price will not". Practitioner heuristic; the direction-conditioned reading is my synthesis of those two definitions.
5. **Unfinished auction at the extreme = the extreme was not rejected cleanly.** "Unfinished edges act as a magnet: price tends to return and complete what it started" (ATAS Learn). Sources disagree on strength: another describes such extremes as "levels the market frequently, but not always, revisits" (search summary of LuxAlgo/others). Practitioner heuristic; usable as a flag on the bar high/low, not as a direction.
6. **Delta vs price divergence.** New price high with a lower delta high (or new low with a weaker negative delta) = weakening aggression (Tradezella glossary; Mu et al. 2010 in 02 §j for the empirical shape: imbalance peaks at the extreme and partly reverts). Practitioner heuristic with partial empirical support.
7. **Volume at the extremes.** Thin extreme rows (little volume on the last one or two levels at the high/low) = rejection/exhaustion ("a new swing extreme printed on a thin extreme row", aggressor-flow-lib); heavy volume at the extreme with no progress = absorption ("big totals with no progress signal absorption", search summary of LuxAlgo/ATAS). Practitioner heuristic. On a range bar the "extreme" is always exactly one range from the other end, so the useful measure is the share of bar volume in the top-k / bottom-k levels and its delta sign (02 §h `D_top/D_bot`).
8. **Time per bar (range bars).** "Even though range bars don't show time explicitly, the rate at which bars appear conveys volatility" (search summary of a range-bar guide); "a slow down bar followed by a fast upbar is a repeated price movement which can then be exploited" — https://www.metalsmine.com/thread/390007-price-behaviour-inside-the-range-bar-indi; NinjaTrader users build "range bar timer" tools from tick timestamps — https://forum.ninjatrader.com/forum/ninjatrader-8/strategy-development/1227411-range-bar-timer. Practitioner heuristic. The user's guide already uses "3× faster / 3× slower than the median of the last 60 bars" and cites Andersen, Dobrev & Schaumburg 2008 for time-to-cross as a volatility measure (INFERRED guide lines 414–416). Reading for imbalance: a stack printed in a fast bar is initiative tape; a stack in a slow bar with no progress is a fight (absorption). My synthesis.
9. **Large-lot participation.** Bookmap's Absorption Indicator marks "large volume traded over short time periods" at a price; ATAS pairs "Big Trades" with "Stacked Imbalance" and "Speed of Tape" in its level-trading article — https://atas.net/exchange-trading/trading-by-levels-with-the-big-trades-stacked-imbalance-and-speed-of-tape-indicators. A footprint "shows executed volume, not resting hidden size, so this infers an iceberg rather than observing one" (search summary). Practitioner heuristic. Quantower exposes `MaxOneTradeVolume` and `AverageSize` per level (1.6), so "the largest single trade inside the stack, relative to the bar's typical trade" is computable without the DOM; whether those members exist in 1.146.7 is UNVERIFIED until compiled.
10. **Effort versus result, quantified.** aggressor-flow-lib: "Effort is bar delta as a z-score against its own recent history. The effort counts as absorbed when price travelled only a small fraction of the normal distance, or moved against the aggressor." Absorption Detector Pro: "big effort (volume), little result (range), and a rejection close", confirmed with lower-timeframe delta — https://www.tradingview.com/script/27t7SHJs-Absorption-Detector-Pro/. Practitioner heuristics; the user's CVD PRO already implements this family (guide §6, INFERRED) and notes that on finished range bars the result is always one range, so only effort and the next bar can discriminate.
11. **Confirm with CVD and structure; never trade the print alone.** NexusFi: read footprints "as a narrative (what happened to bids, whether initiative followed through) rather than as a standalone signal" — https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/advanced-footprint-chart-patterns; LuxAlgo and ATAS disclaimers above. Practitioner consensus.
12. **Conflicts to keep visible.** Whether stacked imbalances at extremes mean continuation or reversal depends on the next bar (sources explicitly disagree: "imbalance forms then reverses quickly. Often marks turning points" vs "the more consecutive imbalances, the higher the probability of continuation"); whether unfinished auctions apply to every bar or only session extremes (sources differ); P/b profiles are "the most contradictory patterns" (ATAS). An indicator should expose the ingredients (stack size, position, direction match, next-bar response, duration, extreme volume) rather than collapse them into one unexplained score.

What none of the found products does (my inference from the table; the basis for a genuinely different design):
1. Treat the next bar's response as part of the measurement: a stack is "pending" until price either trades through it (held → continuation) or stalls against it (absorbed), and the indicator shows that state change, not just the print (rule 2).
2. Use range-bar geometry: on a finished range bar the result is fixed, so strength = effort per range (delta, volume, largest trade) plus duration (`TicksRight − TicksLeft`, 1.5), ranked against the bar's own recent history rather than fixed multiples (the user's 2.0 rank approach, guide line 453).
3. Condition the reading on where the stack sits (top/bottom k levels vs body) AND on whether it agrees with the bar's direction and close location (rules 3–4), and keep those as separately visible ingredients.
4. Use per-level `MaxOneTradeVolume` / `AverageSize` for large-lot participation inside the stack (rule 9), which the TradingView scripts cannot (no true bid/ask or trade-size data: "TradingView does not provide true exchange Bid/Ask volume data").
5. Flag the unfinished-auction state at the bar extreme and let it age (revisited or not) instead of drawing rays.
6. Guard explicitly for Quantower's data path: phantom/zero-volume range bars (1.2), `TicksRight == 0` on the forming bar (1.5), pure-tick charts (zero duration), and the Delta Dots small-number rules (1.8).
